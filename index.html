The First Rangeway Location

Rangeway Mojave.

The first Rangeway location, on the CA-14 corridor in California's high desert. Off-grid, solar-powered, and opening in 2027.

Why here

The corridor that's been waiting.

The CA-14 corridor connects the Los Angeles basin to the high desert and points north. It is the daily commute for the aerospace and defense workforce at Edwards Air Force Base, Northrop Grumman, Lockheed Martin, Boeing, NASA, and Virgin Galactic, and on weekends, it carries travelers heading to the Eastern Sierra.

It is one of the busiest single corridors in California without a hospitality-led charging stop. The drivers who use it are technically literate, high-income, and over-indexed on EV adoption, and they drive this stretch every day. Right now, they have nowhere to charge.

Rangeway Mojave changes that.

Why Rangeway

The stop should be worth the time.

We think like hotel operators, not utility companies. The stop should be worth the time.

Rangeway is America's first hospitality-driven EV charging network. We are not building more chargers in more parking lots. We are building destinations where the stop itself becomes part of the journey.

Every Rangeway location is designed from the perspective of the person who just got out of the car. What do they need? What would make them feel welcome? What would make them want to come back?

Rangeway Mojave is the first location in a planned network along America's highways, focused on routes where drivers need reliable charging and comfortable stops during extended travel. Every network starts with one site. This is ours.

"In a desert corridor where summer afternoons run hot enough to make charging miserable in any open lot, indoor comfort is not an amenity, it is the product. That is what we are building toward at every Rangeway location."

Zak Winnick, Founder and CEO

Phase I

Built from the ground up, powered by the sun.

Charging under bamboo

An open-bay charging plaza beneath a custom canopy of structurally engineered bamboo, where pulling in feels like pulling up to a property, not a parking lot.

Solar that's already running

On-site solar generation and battery energy storage, integrated into a single off-grid platform that Carbon DC already operates at sites across North America.

Food from our food truck

Fresh food and drinks served from our on-site electric food truck, because you deserve more than a vending machine while you wait.

A lounge from day one

A climate-controlled mobile lounge, solar-powered and built for the desert, giving drivers a comfortable place to sit, cool off, and wait from the day we open.

A site shaped by place

Native high-desert landscaping, shaded seating that holds the afternoon, and a layout designed to read as part of the Mojave Desert, not bolted onto it.

Phase II grows the mobile lounge into the full Basecamp experience: a permanent, climate-controlled building with a staffed cafe, comfortable seating, comfortable restrooms, and on-site coffee roasting from Red Canyon Roasting Company. The lounge grows with the site.

How it works

Sunlight in, electrons out, grid optional.

/01

Solar panels generate the power.

A ground-mounted solar array captures Mojave sunlight across several acres of dedicated solar field, in one of the strongest solar-resource areas in the United States.

/02

Batteries store it.

A battery energy storage system holds the energy and manages load, ensuring consistent charging power regardless of time of day or cloud cover.

/03

Chargers deliver it.

Ultra-fast DC charging ports dispense the stored solar energy directly to your vehicle, and the entire system operates independently of the electrical grid.

This is not experimental technology. Carbon DC's off-grid charging systems are already operating at sites across North America. We are deploying a proven platform, not testing a prototype.
